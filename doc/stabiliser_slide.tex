{\large\bfseries The zero-sum sheath stabiliser}\\[2pt]
{\small ported from SOLPS-ITER \code{b2stbc\_stab\_coeff\_sheath\_te/ti/ni}}

\vspace{6pt}\hrule\vspace{8pt}

\textbf{The problem.} With the floating potential $\Phi=3T_e$, the wall $E\times B$ drift
makes the boundary $n_e$ and $T_e$ overshoot to negative values in 2--3 cells in a
\emph{single} time step. Seven different formulations of the drift-corrected parallel-flow
condition all failed this way.

\vspace{6pt}
\textbf{The fix.} Add to the sheath boundary source of $n_e$, $T_i$, $T_e$:
\[
S^{\rm stab} \;=\; \alpha\;\Pi\;\bigl(X^{\rm old}-X^{\rm new}\bigr),
\qquad
\Pi=\text{the sheath flux factor } \rho\,c_s|b_n|\;\text{already in the term}
\]
In practice: \emph{use $(\gamma_{\rm sheath}+\alpha)$ in the Jacobian, $\gamma_{\rm sheath}$ in
the residual.}

\vspace{6pt}
\textbf{Why it is safe:}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textcolor{blue!55!black}{\textbf{Zero-sum}} --- vanishes identically when
        $X^{\rm new}=X^{\rm old}$, so the \textbf{steady state is unchanged}. It damps only the
        \emph{approach} to equilibrium, not the answer.
  \item Pure implicit diagonal: makes the boundary row stiffer against a per-step jump by a
        factor $1+\alpha/(\gamma_{\rm sheath}-1)$.
  \item $\alpha$ is \textbf{dimensionless} --- a multiple of the sheath transmission
        coefficient, not a physical scale. SOLPS recommends $\alpha=1\!-\!100$; default $0$
        reproduces previous results bit-for-bit.
  \item It damps the \textbf{transport} rows ($n_e,T_i,T_e$), not the momentum condition ---
        SOLPS ships no such term for \code{BCMOM}. This matches the observed failure, where
        $n_e$ and $T_e$ lose positivity together in the same cells.
\end{itemize}

\vspace{6pt}
\textbf{Result.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Drift-enabled run: previously died at $\le 649$ steps $\;\rightarrow\;$
        \textbf{now past 1100 and converging.}
  \item All trends correct: min.\ wall density flat, $|v_{E,n}|$ falling, and the decrements of
        min.\ wall $T_e$ \emph{shrinking} --- asymptotic approach, not collapse.
\end{itemize}

\vspace{6pt}
\textbf{Caveat.} SOLPS iterates within a time step, so there the term is purely a convergence
aid. JOREK takes one solve per step, so it also slows genuine transients. The stationary state
is still unaffected.
