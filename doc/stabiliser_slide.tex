{\large\bfseries The zero-sum sheath stabiliser, spelled out}\\[1pt]
{\footnotesize from SOLPS-ITER \code{b2stbc\_stab\_coeff\_sheath\_te/ti/ni}}
\vspace{3pt}\hrule\vspace{6pt}

\textbf{Step 1 --- the equation.} The wall temperature is heated at rate $Q$ and drained by the
sheath at rate $k\,T$:
\[
\frac{\mathrm{d}T}{\mathrm{d}t}=Q-kT
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
\text{equilibrium when nothing changes:}\quad 0=Q-kT^{*}\quad\Rightarrow\quad \boxed{T^{*}=Q/k}
\]

\textbf{Step 2 --- one implicit time step.} $\Told$ = the value we have; $\Tnew$ = the value we
solve for. ``Implicit'' means the sink uses $\Tnew$:
\[
\frac{\Tnew-\Told}{\Delta t}=Q-k\,\Tnew
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
\Tnew=\frac{\Told+Q\Delta t}{1+k\Delta t}
\]

\textbf{Step 3 --- add the stabiliser.} We add one extra term to the source:
\[
S^{\rm stab}=\alpha k\bigl(\Told-\Tnew\bigr)
\]
\textcolor{hl}{\textbf{Read it carefully: it is the difference between the old and the new value.}}
While $T$ is still changing it is non-zero and resists the change. Once $T$ settles,
$\Tnew=\Told$ and the term is \textbf{exactly zero} --- it switches itself off.

\vspace{3pt}
Putting it in and collecting $\Tnew$ on the left:
\[
\frac{\Tnew-\Told}{\Delta t}=Q-k\Tnew+\alpha k(\Told-\Tnew)
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
\Tnew=\frac{\Told\,(1+\alpha k\Delta t)+Q\Delta t}{1+k\Delta t+\alpha k\Delta t}
\]

\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=blue!3,colframe=hl!60,boxrule=0.6pt,left=6pt,right=6pt,top=4pt,bottom=4pt]
\textbf{Step 4 --- does it move the answer? Set $\Tnew=\Told=T^{*}$ and see.}
\[
T^{*}(1+k\Delta t+\alpha k\Delta t)=T^{*}(1+\alpha k\Delta t)+Q\Delta t
\]
\[
T^{*}+k\Delta t\,T^{*}+\underbrace{\alpha k\Delta t\,T^{*}}_{\text{cancels}}
= T^{*}+\underbrace{\alpha k\Delta t\,T^{*}}_{\text{cancels}}+Q\Delta t
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
k\Delta t\,T^{*}=Q\Delta t
\]
\[
\boxed{\,T^{*}=Q/k\,}\qquad\textcolor{hl}{\textbf{every $\alpha$ term cancelled --- the answer is
unchanged.}}
\]
\end{tcolorbox}

\textbf{Step 5 --- so what \emph{does} it change? The step size.} Same equation, real numbers
($Q=5$, $k=2$, $\Delta t=0.5$, start at $T=9$, so $T^{*}=2.5$):

\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{lccccccc c}
\toprule
& start & \multicolumn{6}{c}{after step $\ldots$} & after 4000 \\
\cmidrule(lr){3-8}
& & 1 & 2 & 3 & 4 & 5 & 6 & \\
\midrule
$\alpha=0$   & 9.000 & 5.750 & 4.125 & 3.313 & 2.906 & 2.703 & 2.602 & \textbf{2.5000000000}\\
$\alpha=100$ & 9.000 & 8.936 & 8.873 & 8.811 & 8.749 & 8.688 & 8.627 & \textbf{2.5000000000}\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\vspace{-2pt}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Both columns end at \textbf{exactly the same} $T^{*}=2.5$.
  \item $\alpha=100$ simply takes \emph{smaller bites}: no single step can overshoot.
  \item That is the entire mechanism. \textbf{Same destination, smaller steps.}
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Step 6 --- why this is not just ``adding damping to make it stable''.} In an implicit
solve there are two separate objects:
\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{ll}
\textbf{residual} & ``how far am I from balance?'' --- the answer is where this is \textbf{zero}\\
\textbf{Jacobian} & ``how big a step do I take?'' --- \textbf{does not appear} in that condition\\
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
$S^{\rm stab}$ is zero at balance, so it adds \textbf{nothing} to the residual; its slope
$-\alpha k$ is not zero, so it adds to the Jacobian. It changes only the second column.
In the code this is literally: \;$(\gamma_{\rm sheath}-1+\alpha)$ in the \textbf{Jacobian},
$(\gamma_{\rm sheath}-1)$ in the \textbf{residual}.

\vspace{2pt}
\textcolor{red!60!black}{\textbf{Careful:}} if we had changed \emph{both}, we would simply have
increased $\gamma_{\rm sheath}$ --- a different sheath, a different answer. Only the Jacobian is
touched.

\vspace{5pt}
\textbf{Step 7 --- and $\alpha$ is not a free fudge factor.} It is dimensionless, a multiple of the
sheath transmission coefficient already in the term. SOLPS ships it, recommends $1$--$100$,
defaults to $0$; $\alpha=0$ reproduces our earlier results bit-for-bit. It damps the
\textbf{transport} rows ($n_e,T_i,T_e$) --- SOLPS ships none for the momentum condition --- which
matches our failure, where $n_e$ and $T_e$ went negative \emph{together} in the same 2--3 cells.

\vspace{4pt}
\textbf{Result:} drift-enabled run previously died at $\le649$ steps
$\;\rightarrow\;$ \textbf{now past 1100 and converging.}

\vspace{3pt}
{\footnotesize\textcolor{black!55}{Two honest notes. (i) The scalar example above demonstrates the
\emph{property} --- same fixed point, smaller steps --- not the instability itself, which comes
from the coupling between equations. (ii) SOLPS iterates inside a time step, so there the term
vanishes before the step is accepted and is purely a convergence aid; JOREK takes one solve per
step, so $\Told$ is the previous \emph{time step} and real transients are slowed too. The
stationary state is unaffected either way.}}
